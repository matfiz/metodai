Lecture 36

Sturm–Liouville problems and eigenfunction expansions

Lecture objectives

We define regular Sturm–Liouville problems, prove Lagrange’s identity and deduce that eigenvalues are real, that eigenfunctions of different eigenvalues are orthogonal for a weight, and that each eigenvalue of a problem with separated boundary conditions is simple. We state the completeness theorem with its hypotheses and expand a function in the eigenfunctions of a problem with a variable coefficient and a weight.

36.2 Real eigenvalues, orthogonality and simplicity

The following theorem is the analogue of LA 9’s statements on self-adjoint operators: real eigenvalues and orthogonal eigenvectors.

Summary

A regular Sturm–Liouville problem has p∈C1,p\in C^1p∈C1, p>0,p>0p>0, continuous qqq and w>0w>0w>0 on a closed interval, and one real boundary condition at each end. Lagrange’s identity makes A\odeopA symmetric on functions with these conditions. Symmetry gives real eigenvalues and orthogonal eigenfunctions for the weight w,ww, and the Wronskian at one end gives simple eigenvalues. Periodic conditions and problems in several variables can have repeated eigenvalues. The completeness theorem gives mean-square expansions of piecewise continuous functions and, under its sign conditions, uniform expansions of continuous, piecewise C1C^1C1 functions that vanish at the Dirichlet ends.
