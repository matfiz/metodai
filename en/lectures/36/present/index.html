Lecture 36

Sturm–Liouville problems and eigenfunction expansions

We define regular Sturm–Liouville problems, prove Lagrange’s identity and deduce that eigenvalues are real, that eigenfunctions of different eigenvalues are orthogonal for a weight, and that each eigenvalue of a problem with separated boundary conditions is simple. We state the completeness theorem with its hypotheses and expand a function in the eigenfunctions of a problem with a variable coefficient and a weight.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Write an eigenvalue problem in Sturm–Liouville form and identify ppp, qqq, www and the boundary conditions.
  • Prove reality, weighted orthogonality and simplicity from Lagrange’s identity and the Wronskian.
  • State the completeness theorem, decide which convergence it gives for a given function, and compute expansion coefficients.
  • Orthogonalize a finite list of functions for a weight, and distinguish periodic conditions and several variables from separated problems in one variable.

Lecture 32 expanded the data of a string in the eigenfunctions sin⁡(knx)\sin(k_nx)sin(kn​x) of −y′′=λy-y''=\lambda y−y′′=λy. A rod whose conductivity varies along its length, or an end that exchanges heat with its surroundings, leads to other eigenvalue problems, which have no explicit solutions in general. In this lecture we prove the symmetry properties of their eigenfunctions and state their completeness. The lecture reads in three stages: the problem and its symmetry (Section 36.1), the proved properties of eigenvalues and eigenfunctions (Section 36.2), and completeness with a worked expansion (Sections 36.3–36.4).

36.2 Real eigenvalues, orthogonality and simplicity

The following theorem is the analogue of LA 9’s statements on self-adjoint operators: real eigenvalues and orthogonal eigenvectors.

  • Reality. Every eigenvalue is real, and for every eigenvalue there is a real eigenfunction.

Summary

A regular Sturm–Liouville problem has p∈C1p\in C^1p∈C1, p>0p>0p>0, continuous qqq and w>0w>0w>0 on a closed interval, and one real boundary condition at each end. Lagrange’s identity makes A\odeopA symmetric on functions with these conditions. Symmetry gives real eigenvalues and orthogonal eigenfunctions for the weight www, and the Wronskian at one end gives simple eigenvalues. Periodic conditions and problems in several variables can have repeated eigenvalues. The completeness theorem gives mean-square expansions of piecewise continuous functions and, under its sign conditions, uniform expansions of continuous, piecewise C1C^1C1 functions that vanish at the Dirichlet ends.

  • Lagrange’s identity: ⟨u,Av⟩w−⟨Au,v⟩w=[p(u′‾v−u‾v′)]ab\inner u{\odeop v}_w-\inner{\odeop u}v_w=[p(\conj{u'}v-\conj uv')]_a^b⟨u,Av⟩w​−⟨Au,v⟩w​=[p(u′v−uv′)]ab​, zero on the domain.

  • Properties: real eigenvalues, ⟨y,z⟩w=0\inner{y}{z}_w=0⟨y,z⟩w​=0 for different eigenvalues, simplicity for separated conditions.
