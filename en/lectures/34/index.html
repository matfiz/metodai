Lecture 34

Complex potentials for plane fields and flows

Lecture objectives

We define the complex potential of a plane field and recover the field, its equipotentials and its field lines from it. We build fields from elementary analytic functions, locate their critical and singular points, solve the flow past a cylinder, and measure circulation and flux by contour integrals.

Lecture 33 solved Laplace’s equation on a rectangle by series. In the plane there is a second route. Lecture 15 showed that the real and imaginary parts of an analytic function are harmonic, so every analytic function gives two harmonic functions at once. In this lecture we read a plane field from one analytic function: its potential, its field lines and its strength. The lecture reads in three stages: the complex potential and its fields (Sections 34.1–34.2), critical points and the flow past a cylinder (Sections 34.3–34.4), and circulation, flux and global potentials (Section 34.5).

34.1 The complex potential

Throughout the lecture U⊂CU\subset\CU⊂C is open and z=x+iy=reiθ.z=x+\iu y=r\ee^{\iu\theta}z=x+iy=reiθ. The plane polar angle is θ,\thetaθ, as in Lectures 15–16 (Lecture 2 wrote φ\varphiφ for it), and a real function on UUU is also read as a function of (x,y).(x,y)(x,y).

Definition 34.1 (Complex potential). Let uuu be harmonic on U.UU. A complex potential of uuu is an analytic function Ω=u+iv\Omega=u+\iu vΩ=u+iv on UUU whose real part is u.uu. The function uuu is the potential and vvv is the stream function.

Here Ω\OmegaΩ is a function, not the open set of Lecture 22’s test functions. The stream function is a harmonic conjugate of uuu in the sense of Lecture 15. On a star-shaped set it exists and is unique up to an additive real constant (Lecture 15’s theorem on the harmonic conjugate on a star-shaped set). On a set with a hole it may fail to exist, as for the temperature ln⁡r\ln rlnr of the cylindrical shell in Lecture 15. Section 34.5 explains this failure in physical terms. By Lecture 17’s proposition that analytic functions are infinitely differentiable, uuu and vvv have continuous partial derivatives of every order.

34.3 Critical and singular points

The examples have two kinds of special points. At the corner of Az2Az^2Az2 the velocity vanishes. At the centre of a source, vortex or dipole the function Ω\OmegaΩ is not analytic and the field is unbounded.

Summary

A complex potential Ω=u+iv\Omega=u+\iu vΩ=u+iv is an analytic function whose real part is a plane harmonic potential u,uu, and its derivative gives the field. Walls and insulated boundaries are streamlines, and conductors and isotherms are equipotentials. The source, vortex and dipole have unbounded fields near their singular points. Nonzero flux forbids a stream function on the whole region, and nonzero circulation a potential on the whole region.
