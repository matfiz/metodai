Lecture 34

Complex potentials for plane fields and flows

We define the complex potential of a plane field and recover the field, its equipotentials and its field lines from it. We build fields from elementary analytic functions, locate their critical and singular points, solve the flow past a cylinder, and measure circulation and flux by contour integrals.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Recover the field or velocity components from a complex potential, with the convention and sign of the physical problem.
  • Draw the equipotentials and field lines of an elementary potential and identify its physical region, its critical points and the singular points excluded from it.
  • Check that a curve is a wall or a conductor of a given flow or field, and compute speeds and stagnation points.
  • Compute circulation and flux from a contour integral and decide whether a field has a potential or a stream function on the whole region.

Lecture 33 solved Laplace’s equation on a rectangle by series. In the plane there is a second route. Lecture 15 showed that the real and imaginary parts of an analytic function are harmonic, so every analytic function gives two harmonic functions at once. In this lecture we read a plane field from one analytic function: its potential, its field lines and its strength. The lecture reads in three stages: the complex potential and its fields (Sections 34.1–34.2), critical points and the flow past a cylinder (Sections 34.3–34.4), and circulation, flux and global potentials (Section 34.5).

34.1 The complex potential

Throughout the lecture U⊂CU\subset\CU⊂C is open and z=x+iy=reiθz=x+\iu y=r\ee^{\iu\theta}z=x+iy=reiθ. The plane polar angle is θ\thetaθ, as in Lectures 15–16 (Lecture 2 wrote φ\varphiφ for it), and a real function on UUU is also read as a function of (x,y)(x,y)(x,y).

Definition 34.1 (Complex potential). Let uuu be harmonic on UUU. A complex potential of uuu is an analytic function Ω=u+iv\Omega=u+\iu vΩ=u+iv on UUU whose real part is uuu. The function uuu is the potential and vvv is the stream function.

Here Ω\OmegaΩ is a function, not the open set of Lecture 22’s test functions. The stream function is a harmonic conjugate of uuu in the sense of Lecture 15. On a star-shaped set it exists and is unique up to an additive real constant (Lecture 15’s theorem on the harmonic conjugate on a star-shaped set). On a set with a hole it may fail to exist, as for the temperature ln⁡r\ln rlnr of the cylindrical shell in Lecture 15. Section 34.5 explains this failure in physical terms. By Lecture 17’s proposition that analytic functions are infinitely differentiable, uuu and vvv have continuous partial derivatives of every order.

The same analytic function serves three physical problems. Each reads the gradient with its own sign. A plane field does not depend on the coordinate along the third axis. A quantity per unit length along that axis is said to be per unit depth.

  • Potential flow. 34.3 Critical and singular points

The examples have two kinds of special points. At the corner of Az2Az^2Az2 the velocity vanishes. At the centre of a source, vortex or dipole the function Ω\OmegaΩ is not analytic and the field is unbounded.

Definition 34.4 (Critical and singular points). A point z0∈Uz_0\in Uz0​∈U with Ω′(z0)=0\Omega'(z_0)=0Ω′(z0​)=0 is a critical point of Ω\OmegaΩ. In a flow it is a stagnation point. 34.5 Circulation and flux

We first name the quantities that measure how a plane field turns around a closed path and how much of it crosses the path, and the functions whose existence on a whole region they decide.

In this lecture each potential was given, and its walls and conductors were found among its level curves. In a boundary-value problem the region and the boundary data are given, and the potential is sought. Lecture 35 finds it by mapping the region onto a simpler one.

Summary

A complex potential Ω=u+iv\Omega=u+\iu vΩ=u+iv is an analytic function whose real part is a plane harmonic potential uuu, and its derivative gives the field. Walls and insulated boundaries are streamlines, and conductors and isotherms are equipotentials. The source, vortex and dipole have unbounded fields near their singular points. Nonzero flux forbids a stream function on the whole region, and nonzero circulation a potential on the whole region.
