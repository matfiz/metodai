Linear equations, exact differentials and integrating factors

Lecture objectives

We describe the solution set of a linear equation and derive its solution by variation of constants. We solve exact equations through a potential, state the domain on which a potential exists, and construct integrating factors that make an inexact equation exact.

GM 33 solved the first-order linear equation with an integrating factor. In this lecture we first derive the same solution by variation of constants, which builds it from a homogeneous solution. This method extends to second-order equations and to systems in later lectures. Next we write a first-order equation as a differential form. A form that is the differential of a function gives its solutions as level curves. An integrating factor turns other forms, including the linear one, into such differentials.

Summary

The solutions of a linear equation with continuous coefficients exist on the whole interval of the coefficients. They form a particular solution plus a one-dimensional space of homogeneous solutions. Variation of constants finds a particular solution from any homogeneous solution without zeros. The linear and the scaling meanings of “homogeneous” describe different invariances.

In an exact equation the left-hand side is the differential of a potential, and away from critical points its solution curves are level curves. The cross-partial test proves exactness on a star-shaped domain. On other domains it gives local potentials, which describe each solution near every point. A single relation for all solutions needs a global potential. On a rectangle where N≠0,N\ne0N=0, an integrating factor depending on xxx alone exists exactly when (∂yM−∂xN)/N(\partial_yM-\partial_xN)/N(∂y​M−∂x​N)/N depends on xxx alone.

  • Linear solution set: yp+Ce−Py_{\mathrm p}+C\ee^{-P}yp​+Ce−P on the whole coefficient interval.

  • Variation of constants: y=C(x)yhy=C(x)y_{\mathrm h}y=C(x)yh​ with C′=q/yh.C'=q/y_{\mathrm h}C′=q/yh​.

  • Exactness test: ∂yM=∂xN\partial_yM=\partial_xN∂y​M=∂x​N on a star-shaped domain.

  • Integrating factor μ(x):\mu(x)μ(x): on a rectangle with N≠0,N\ne0N=0, exists when (∂yM−∂xN)/N(\partial_yM-\partial_xN)/N(∂y​M−∂x​N)/N depends on xxx only.

  • Local and global: equal cross partial derivatives on the punctured plane do not give a potential there.
