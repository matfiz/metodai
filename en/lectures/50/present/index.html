Lecture 50

Field action and the variational string equation

We construct the small-slope string action from energies and derive its field equation for a C2C^2C2 displacement. We retain all boundary terms to distinguish fixed configurations in time from fixed or free spatial ends.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Derive the bead equations and state what their continuum consistency means.
  • Derive the field first variation with all four edge contributions.
  • Recover the forced string equation and the natural condition at a free end.
  • Distinguish the fixed temporal configurations of Hamilton’s principle from Cauchy data.

Lecture 29 derived the string equation from force balance and promised a derivation from its action. We now give that derivation. The stages are the passage from beads to a field, variation in space and time, and the string equation with its end conditions and initial data.

Summary

A bead chain motivates a continuous displacement field. The small-slope string action gives the same forced equation as Lecture 29’s force balance. For a C2C^2C2 field, integration by parts and the fundamental lemma turn stationarity into an interior PDE and conditions on the free edges.

  • Field action: the density depends on uuu, uxu_xux​ and utu_tut​. Each derivative term contributes its own pair of boundary edges.

  • String equation: for a C2C^2C2 field and continuous forcing, stationarity under directions vanishing on ∂R\partial R∂R is equivalent to ρutt=Tuxx+f\rho u_{tt}=Tu_{xx}+fρutt​=Tuxx​+f in the small-slope model.

  • Spatial ends: essential conditions prescribe displacement and restrict the directions. A free massless end gives the natural condition ux=0u_x=0ux​=0.

  • Temporal edges: fixed configurations remove temporal boundary terms. Cauchy displacement and velocity are prescribed separately.
