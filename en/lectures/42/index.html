Lecture 42

Green’s functions for ODEs

Lecture objectives

We construct the Green’s function of a second-order problem with one boundary condition at each end from two homogeneous solutions and their Wronskian, and prove that it exists exactly when the homogeneous problem has only the zero solution. We derive its jump condition, compare it with the causal kernel of an oscillator, and treat a problem with a nonzero homogeneous solution through a compatibility condition and a generalized inverse.

Lecture 8 wrote the solution of a forced linear equation with zero initial data as an integral of the forcing against a kernel, and Lecture 28 turned that kernel into the causal response of an oscillator. A string fixed at both ends, a rod held at two temperatures or a trajectory with prescribed positions at two times carries one condition at each end of an interval instead of two conditions at one end. In this lecture we build the kernels of such problems, the Green’s functions, and find when they exist. The lecture reads in three stages: the problem and the construction (Section 42.1), the jump condition and two kernels for one oscillator (Sections 42.2–42.3), and problems whose homogeneous version has nonzero solutions, with the expansion in eigenfunctions (Sections 42.4–42.5).
