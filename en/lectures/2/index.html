A radial flow crosses cylindrical surfaces whose areas increase with radius. Its speed may decrease while the total outward flow remains constant. Flux sums the field’s normal component over a surface. Circulation is the signed line integral around a closed curve. Divergence measures the local flux balance. Curl measures circulation per unit area. We derive their coordinate formulas from the geometry of Lecture 1.

2.1 Cartesian definitions and coordinate preparation

Summary

Divergence differentiates physical components multiplied by the scale-factor products that measure coordinate-face area. Curl differentiates physical components multiplied by the scale factors that measure coordinate-edge length. The scale factors therefore express local flux and circulation geometry. In cylindrical coordinates, direct differentiation of the moving basis reproduces both formulas. Applying divergence to a scalar gradient gives the cylindrical Laplacian; for a field depending on rrr alone only its radial term remains. Solving the source-free radial boundary-value problem then gives the logarithmic steady temperature profile.

  • Divergence: H−1∑i∂i(HAi/hi)H^{-1}\sum_i\partial_i(HA_i/h_i)H−1∑i​∂i​(HAi​/hi​) on an orthogonal patch.

  • Curl: line-weighted components with right-hand orientation.

  • Moving basis: coordinate derivatives act on basis vectors as well as coefficients.

  • Radial cylindrical scalar Laplacian: f′′+f′/rf''+f'/rf′′+f′/r on r>0.r>0r>0.

  • Integral laws: verify field regularity on the entire region or surface.
