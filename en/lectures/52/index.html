Lecture 52

Steepest descent and Stirling’s formula

Lecture objectives

We reduce an integral near a simple complex saddle to a Gaussian and retain the factor supplied by the path’s orientation. We distinguish this local calculation from the global estimates needed to change the contour.

Lecture 51 obtained leading terms from a real maximum or a real stationary point. Analyticity lets us move an integration path to pass through a saddle point in the complex plane. The stages are geometry and the theorem (Sections 52.1–52.2), Stirling’s formula (Section 52.3), a justified contour deformation (Section 52.4), and limitations (Section 52.5).

Remark 52.3 (Before deforming a contour). To replace CCC by another contour, check analyticity of geΛfg\ee^{\Lambda f}geΛf throughout the swept region. 52.5 Limits of a local approximation

For a contour containing finitely many separated simple saddles, local contributions can be added if each has a segment of the stated kind and the remaining contour has a positive gap below all their values of Re⁡f,\Re fRef, with the required weighted integrability. Split the integral into those segments and the remainder, then apply the same local estimates and gap bound to each piece. Contributions of equal exponential size can cancel through their phase factors. An endpoint or singularity with a larger real phase also needs its own analysis before any saddle term is called leading.

As a parameter varies, the contours that justify a decomposition into saddle contributions can change. The appearance or disappearance of an exponentially small term in such descriptions is called a Stokes phenomenon. This is an outlook, not a uniform approximation theorem.

Summary

An analytic phase permits a contour deformation, but the equality of improper integrals requires estimates at their ends. At a simple saddle, a straight descent segment converts the quadratic exponent to a real Gaussian. A gap and weighted absolute integrability control the rest.

  • Local geometry: the argument of f′′(z∗)f''(z_*)f′′(z∗​) determines the descent directions, while  ⁣dz\dd zdz supplies the orientation factor.

  • Saddle-point method: for a fixed contour satisfying the theorem’s hypotheses, the Gaussian term has an additive bracket error O ⁣(Λ−1)\bigO{\Lambda^{-1}}O(Λ−1) as real Λ→+∞,\Lambda\to+\inftyΛ→+∞, with the dependencies in the theorem.

  • Applications: the positive real Gamma integral gives Stirling’s leading formula. An upward contour shift reveals exponential smallness in the cubic oscillatory integral.

  • Scope: a local expansion alone justifies neither a contour deformation nor uniformity as saddles or parameters move.
