Lecture 38

Series solutions and the Frobenius method

Lecture objectives

We prove that at an ordinary point every solution is a power series whose radius is at least that of the coefficients, and we solve Legendre’s equation this way. At a regular singular point we derive the indicial equation, prove that the exponent with the larger real part gives a solution xαx^{\alpha}xα times a power series, and find a second solution, which may contain a logarithm when the exponents differ by a nonnegative integer.

Lecture 7 solved Euler equations exactly and found second solutions by reduction of order. Most linear equations with variable coefficients have no solutions in closed form. The eigenvalue problems of Lectures 39 and 40 lead to Bessel’s and Legendre’s equations, whose coefficients are rational functions. In this lecture we construct the solutions of such equations as power series. The lecture reads in three stages: ordinary points and Legendre’s equation (Sections 38.1–38.2), Frobenius series at a regular singular point (Section 38.3), and the second solution when the exponents differ by an integer (Section 38.4).
