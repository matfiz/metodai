Lecture 9

Mechanical oscillators and physical response

We derive the oscillator equation and interpret its parameters through the decay rate of free motion. We solve the problem of a sinusoidal force switched on at rest, separate the transient from the steady response, and distinguish the exact resonant solution from its undamped limit and from its weak-damping approximation. The energy balance gives the mean absorbed power, and the same equation describes a series circuit.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Derive the oscillator equation from the forces and interpret ω0\omega_0ω0​, γ\gammaγ and the decay rate.
  • Solve a sinusoidally forced initial-value problem with γ>0\gamma>0γ>0 and separate its transient and steady parts.
  • Distinguish an exact solution, a limit and an approximation of the resonant response, with their ranges of validity.
  • Compute the mean absorbed power from the energy balance and transfer the results to a series circuit.

The damped, driven oscillator is a basic physical model described by a second-order linear equation. GM 34 and GM 35 found its free motions and its steady response to a sinusoidal force. Lecture 8 supplies the general tools: undetermined coefficients for that steady response and the kernel for any continuous force. In this lecture we derive the model from the forces and solve the complete initial-value problem of a force that is switched on. Its solution separates into a transient and a steady response. At the natural frequency we compare the exact solution with its undamped limit and with an approximation for weak damping. Finally, we follow the energy and translate the results to an electric circuit.

Summary

The oscillator equation has two rates, the natural frequency ω0\omega_0ω0​ and the damping rate γ\gammaγ. Free motion decays at the rate κ\kappaκ, which is largest at critical damping. For γ>0\gamma>0γ>0, a sinusoidal force switched on at rest produces the steady response plus a transient free motion fixed by the initial data, and for 0<γ<ω00<\gamma<\omega_00<γ<ω0​ the transient decays like e−γt\ee^{-\gamma t}e−γt.
