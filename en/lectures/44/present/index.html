Lecture 44

Probability currents and the Fokker–Planck equation

We compute probabilities and moments from densities, then derive the weak forward equation from assumptions on small random displacements. We state the smoothness needed for a classical PDE, and verify the equation and boundary flow for constant drift.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Compute probabilities and averages from a nonnegative function of integral one.
  • Derive the weak forward equation under the stated increment bounds.
  • Recover the differential equation under the stated smoothness conditions.
  • Verify the translating Gaussian and distinguish the initial density from probability crossing a boundary.

Lecture 43 propagated a unit amount by a diffusion kernel. We now read the amount in an interval as the probability of finding a particle there. The stages are probability preparation (Sections 44.1–44.2), short increments and their weak forward equation (Section 44.3), and flow across boundaries with a translating Gaussian (Sections 44.4–44.5).
