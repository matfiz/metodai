Parts I and II solved mainly ordinary differential equations. A vibrating string and a cooling rod need functions of position and time together, and their laws are partial differential equations. In this first lecture of Part III we derive the wave equation of a string and the heat equation of a rod from balance laws on a small element, and we state which initial and boundary data a physical problem supplies. We then sort second-order equations into three types, which take different kinds of data, and survey the equations of these types across fields. The lecture reads in three stages: the two models (Sections 29.1–29.3), the data and well-posedness (Section 29.4), and the types, the survey and the action (Sections 29.5–29.7).
