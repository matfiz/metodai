Spherical coordinates describe a point by its distance from the origin and two angles. They are useful when a boundary is a sphere or a field depends only on distance. The coordinate basis changes direction with both angles. We use the geometry of Lecture 1 and the operator formulas of Lecture 2 to calculate derivatives in this basis.

3.1 The spherical coordinate patch

Definition 3.1 (Spherical coordinates). The spherical position map is given by r(r,θ,φ)=(rsin⁡θcos⁡φ, rsin⁡θsin⁡φ, rcos⁡θ).\vv r(r,\theta,\varphi) =(r\sin\theta\cos\varphi,\ r\sin\theta\sin\varphi,\ r\cos\theta).r(r,θ,φ)=(rsinθcosφ, rsinθsinφ, rcosθ).(3.1) Its coordinates are the distance rrr from the origin, the polar angle θ\thetaθ from the positive zzz-axis, and the azimuth φ\varphiφ from the positive xxx-axis in the xyxyxy-plane. Our working parameter domain is U=(0,∞)×(0,π)×(−π,π).U=(0,\infty)\times(0,\pi)\times(-\pi,\pi).U=(0,∞)×(0,π)×(−π,π). The map sends UUU into R3.\R^3R3.

The order is (r,θ,φ).(r,\theta,\varphi)(r,θ,φ). Interchanging the two angles changes the orientation of the ordered coordinate basis. The radius in this lecture is the three-dimensional distance. The cylindrical radius of Lectures 1–2 was distance from the zzz-axis.

The image of this patch excludes the zzz-axis and the azimuth cut where y=0y=0y=0 and x<0.x<0x<0. On its image the inverse is r=x2+y2+z2,θ=arccos⁡(z/r),φ=atan2⁡(y,x)∈(−π,π).r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2},\qquad \theta=\arccos(z/r),\qquad \varphi=\operatorname{atan2}(y,x)\in(-\pi,\pi).r=x2+y2+z2​,θ=arccos(z/r),φ=atan2(y,x)∈(−π,π).(3.2) On this patch, atan2⁡(y,x)\operatorname{atan2}(y,x)atan2(y,x) is the unique angle φ∈(−π,π)\varphi\in(-\pi,\pi)φ∈(−π,π) satisfying (cos⁡φ,sin⁡φ)=(x,y)x2+y2.(\cos\varphi,\sin\varphi)=\frac{(x,y)}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}.(cosφ,sinφ)=x2+y2​(x,y)​. The signs of both Cartesian coordinates therefore determine its quadrant, or its half-axis when one of them vanishes. The single-argument inverse tangent arctan⁡(y/x)\arctan(y/x)arctan(y/x) loses this quadrant information. It is also undefined when x=0.x=0x=0. Equation (3.2) proves injectivity on U.UU. At a point of the positive or negative zzz-axis, the azimuth is undetermined. At the origin neither angle is determined. Changing the azimuth interval moves the cut. It does not remove the polar-axis degeneracy. A second spherical patch with a different polar axis can cover each nonzero point on the original axis.

Summary

The spherical scale factors describe radial lengths, meridian arc lengths and azimuthal arc lengths. Substitution into the general orthogonal formulas gives the spherical gradient, divergence, curl and scalar Laplacian. Basis derivatives provide an independent operator check. Coordinate denominators do not identify physical singularities without a Cartesian or limiting argument.

For spherically symmetric fields, the area factor r2r^2r2 determines both the flux law and the radial harmonic equation. A divergence-free spherically symmetric radial field is inverse-square on its radial interval. A radial harmonic potential is a constant plus an inverse radius. Neither assertion assigns a classical value at a singular origin, and neither extends to arbitrary angular dependence.
