Lecture 45

Ornstein–Uhlenbeck evolution and its Green kernel

We derive the transition density by Fourier transformation and characteristics, and prove uniqueness in the classical solution class specified below. We evolve a Gaussian initial cloud and distinguish its long-time limit from the limit of vanishing restoring force.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Derive the Fourier equation and solve its characteristic equations.
  • Verify the transition density, its initial trace, composition and moments.
  • Evolve Gaussian data, identify the stationary density for positive restoring rate and distinguish it from the transition density.
  • Recover free diffusion at fixed elapsed time as the restoring rate tends to zero.

Lecture 44 described diffusion with a prescribed drift velocity. Here the velocity is proportional to displacement and directed towards the origin. The stages are the Fourier solution (Sections 45.1–45.2), kernel and moments (Section 45.3), and Gaussian relaxation with its stationary and free-diffusion limits (Sections 45.4–45.5).
