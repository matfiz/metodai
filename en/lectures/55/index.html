Lecture 54’s naive expansion grew without bound in time although the motion stays bounded. Here we remove that growth by correcting the frequency. The stages are the exact period, periodic solvability, frequency correction, its error bound, and slow amplitude modulation.

Summary

The Duffing period changes with the small parameter. Periodic solvability fixes the frequency correction that a naive expansion misses. A residual estimate then determines what accuracy has actually been proved.

  • Exact period: the energy integral and turning-point data give (55.2), including its smallest-period interpretation.

  • Solvability: for continuous 2π2\pi2π-periodic h,hh, the equation y′′+y=hy''+y=hy′′+y=h has a 2π2\pi2π-periodic solution exactly when the integrals of hhh against cos⁡θ\cos\thetacosθ and sin⁡θ\sin\thetasinθ over one period vanish.

  • Frequency correction: ω1=3/8\omega_1=3/8ω1​=3/8 removes first-order resonance, while X1X_1X1​ preserves the prescribed data.

  • Error and modulation: the corrected Duffing approximation has error O ⁣(ε)\bigO{\varepsilon}O(ε) as ε→0+\varepsilon\to0^+ε→0+ uniformly on each [0,c/ε],[0,c/\varepsilon][0,c/ε], for fixed c>0.c>0c>0. A decaying oscillation also needs a slow amplitude.
