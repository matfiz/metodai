Lecture 16

Branches, cuts and Riemann surfaces

We define branches of the logarithm and of the powers, prove that they are analytic, and prove that no branch of the logarithm exists around the origin. We follow the argument along a path, count the turns of a closed path, and identify branch points. Finally we build the two-sheeted surface on which the square root is single-valued.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Specify a branch and its domain before computing with log⁡z\log zlogz, zαz^\alphazα or a root, and compute the values of such an expression along a given path.
  • Compute the number of turns of a closed path around a point from a continuous argument.
  • Locate the branch points of an expression built from logarithms and powers, including the point at infinity, and choose cuts that make a branch exist.
  • Distinguish a cut, which belongs to a chosen branch, from an intrinsic singularity, and describe the two-sheeted surface of the square root.

The equation ew=z\ee^w=zew=z has infinitely many solutions www for each z≠0z\ne0z=0, so a logarithm is a choice.
