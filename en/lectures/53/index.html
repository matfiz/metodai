Perturbing algebraic equations

Lecture objectives

Lectures 51–52 expanded integrals as a large parameter grows. Here a small parameter ε→0+\varepsilon\to0^+ε→0+ perturbs an equation, and we expand its roots. The stages are coefficient matching, residual estimates, iteration and a change of scale for an escaping root. The common thread here and in Lectures 54–55 is that successive corrections solve linear problems with the same leading coefficient or operator.

Summary

A simple reduced root makes each matching equation solvable and keeps a derivative bounded away from zero nearby. The residual then bounds the root error. A root that escapes this neighbourhood needs a different scale.

  • Power expansions: their remainder is specified at each fixed order as ε→0+.\varepsilon\to0^+ε→0+. Convergence of the infinite series is separate.

  • Regular roots: a simple polynomial root persists locally. Matching and the derivative bound prove the finite-order expansions.

  • Iteration: an invariant closed interval and a contraction bound prove convergence as the iteration number tends to infinity.

  • Singular roots: balance chooses a scale. Rescaling restores a simple reduced root, and a residual test certifies the original root error.
