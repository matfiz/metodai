Lecture 12

Repeated eigenvalues, matrix exponentials and stability

We prove that the exponentials of commuting matrices multiply, and we use this to compute etA\ee^{tA}etA for a two-by-two matrix with a double eigenvalue. We then classify the long-time behaviour of the solutions by the eigenvalues, treating eigenvalues on the imaginary axis separately.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Distinguish a double eigenvalue with two independent eigenvectors from a defective two-by-two matrix.
  • For a two-by-two matrix with a double eigenvalue λ\lambdaλ, compute etA=eλt(I+tN)\ee^{tA}=\ee^{\lambda t}(I+tN)etA=eλt(I+tN) with N=A−λIN=A-\lambda IN=A−λI, and solve the system with its initial data.
  • Decide asymptotic stability of a two-by-two system from its trace and determinant, and settle the remaining cases from the eigenvalues and eigenvectors.
  • Explain with an example why the eigenvalues alone do not decide stability when a real part vanishes.

Lecture 11 computed the matrix exponential from a basis of eigenvectors. A matrix with a repeated eigenvalue may have no such basis. The critically damped oscillator of Lecture 9 is an example, and its motions contain the factor te−γtt\ee^{-\gamma t}te−γt. In this lecture we compute the exponential of a two-by-two matrix with a repeated eigenvalue. We then decide from the eigenvalues when the solutions of a system with constant coefficients stay bounded or decay, and we identify the cases in which the eigenvalues alone do not decide.

12.5 Stability of linear systems

We describe the long-time behaviour of x˙=Ax\dot{\vv x}=A\vv xx˙=Ax by the following three notions (Teschl, §3.2).

Definition 12.7 (Stability of a linear system). The system x˙=Ax\dot{\vv x}=A\vv xx˙=Ax with a constant real matrix AAA is

  • stable if every real solution is bounded on [0,∞)[0,\infty)[0,∞);

  • asymptotically stable if every real solution tends to 000 as t→∞t\to\inftyt→∞;

  • unstable if it is not stable.

An asymptotically stable system is stable, because a function that tends to 000 and is continuous on [0,∞)[0,\infty)[0,∞) is bounded there. Stability means that solutions with small initial data stay small. If every solution is bounded, so are the columns of etA\ee^{tA}etA, which are solutions. Summary

A two-by-two matrix with a double eigenvalue λ\lambdaλ is either λI\lambda IλI or defective, with one line of eigenvectors. In both cases N=A−λIN=A-\lambda IN=A−λI satisfies N2=0N^2=0N2=0. Exponentials of commuting matrices multiply, so etA=eλt(I+tN)\ee^{tA}=\ee^{\lambda t}(I+tN)etA=eλt(I+tN). A defective two-by-two system therefore has solutions whose term teλtNx0t\ee^{\lambda t}N\vv x_0teλtNx0​ points along the eigenvector line. The critically damped oscillator is such a system. For constant coefficients, negative real parts of all eigenvalues give asymptotic stability, and an eigenvalue with a positive real part gives instability. When the largest real part is zero, stability depends on whether the eigenvalues on the imaginary axis have enough eigenvectors. For a real two-by-two matrix, asymptotic stability is equivalent to tr⁡A<0\tr A<0trA<0 and det⁡A>0\det A>0detA>0.

  • Double eigenvalue: for a two-by-two matrix with double eigenvalue λ\lambdaλ, N=A−λIN=A-\lambda IN=A−λI satisfies N2=0N^2=0N2=0; AAA is defective unless N=0N=0N=0.

  • Commuting exponentials: if AB=BAAB=BAAB=BA, then et(A+B)=etAetB\ee^{t(A+B)}=\ee^{tA}\ee^{tB}et(A+B)=etAetB.

  • Stability: for real two-by-two AAA, asymptotically stable   ⟺  \iff⟺ Re⁡λ1<0\Re\lambda_1<0Reλ1​<0 and Re⁡λ2<0\Re\lambda_2<0Reλ2​<0   ⟺  \iff⟺ tr⁡A<0\tr A<0trA<0 and det⁡A>0\det A>0detA>0; in the marginal cases the eigenvectors decide.
