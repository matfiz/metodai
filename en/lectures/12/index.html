Lecture 12

Repeated eigenvalues, matrix exponentials and stability

Lecture objectives

We prove that the exponentials of commuting matrices multiply, and we use this to compute etA\ee^{tA}etA for a two-by-two matrix with a double eigenvalue. We then classify the long-time behaviour of the solutions by the eigenvalues, treating eigenvalues on the imaginary axis separately.

Lecture 11 computed the matrix exponential from a basis of eigenvectors. A matrix with a repeated eigenvalue may have no such basis. The critically damped oscillator of Lecture 9 is an example, and its motions contain the factor te−γt.t\ee^{-\gamma t}te−γt. In this lecture we compute the exponential of a two-by-two matrix with a repeated eigenvalue. We then decide from the eigenvalues when the solutions of a system with constant coefficients stay bounded or decay, and we identify the cases in which the eigenvalues alone do not decide.

12.1 Double eigenvalues of a two-by-two matrix

In this lecture AAA is a constant real matrix, and III is the identity matrix. Definition 12.1 (Double eigenvalue and defective matrix). A two-by-two matrix AAA has the double (or repeated) eigenvalue λ\lambdaλ if χA(μ)=(μ−λ)2.\chi_A(\mu)=(\mu-\lambda)^2χA​(μ)=(μ−λ)2. A double eigenvalue of a real 2×22\times22×2 matrix is real, because tr⁡A=2λ\tr A=2\lambdatrA=2λ and det⁡A=λ2,\det A=\lambda^2detA=λ2, and tr⁡A\tr AtrA is real. Over C\CC the algebraic multiplicities add up to n,nn, and each geometric multiplicity is at most the algebraic one (LA 5, Proposition “Geometric ≤\le≤ algebraic”).

12.5 Stability of linear systems

We describe the long-time behaviour of x˙=Ax\dot{\vv x}=A\vv xx˙=Ax by the following three notions (Teschl, §3.2).

Definition 12.7 (Stability of a linear system). The system x˙=Ax\dot{\vv x}=A\vv xx˙=Ax with a constant real matrix AAA is

  • stable if every real solution is bounded on [0,∞);[0,\infty)[0,∞);

  • asymptotically stable if every real solution tends to 000 as t→∞;t\to\inftyt→∞;

Summary

A two-by-two matrix with a double eigenvalue λ\lambdaλ is either λI\lambda IλI or defective, with one line of eigenvectors. In both cases N=A−λIN=A-\lambda IN=A−λI satisfies N2=0.N^2=0N2=0. Exponentials of commuting matrices multiply, so etA=eλt(I+tN).\ee^{tA}=\ee^{\lambda t}(I+tN)etA=eλt(I+tN). A defective two-by-two system therefore has solutions whose term teλtNx0t\ee^{\lambda t}N\vv x_0teλtNx0​ points along the eigenvector line. The critically damped oscillator is such a system. For constant coefficients, negative real parts of all eigenvalues give asymptotic stability, and an eigenvalue with a positive real part gives instability. When the largest real part is zero, stability depends on whether the eigenvalues on the imaginary axis have enough eigenvectors. For a real two-by-two matrix, asymptotic stability is equivalent to tr⁡A<0\tr A<0trA<0 and det⁡A>0.\det A>0detA>0.
