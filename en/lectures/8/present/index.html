Lecture 8

Inhomogeneous second-order equations

We derive variation of parameters and write its result as an integral of the forcing against a kernel built from a fundamental system. We prove that the method of undetermined coefficients has exactly one solution of the trial form, with an extra power of xxx at a characteristic root. We solve one problem by both methods and impose initial data on the full solution.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Given a fundamental system, solve a regular inhomogeneous initial-value problem, impose the data on the full solution and verify it.
  • Derive the variation-of-parameters formula and compute its kernel from a fundamental system.
  • For constant coefficients and forcing by polynomials times exponentials, sines or cosines, choose the trial form of undetermined coefficients, including the factor xkx^kxk at a characteristic root.
  • Decide which method applies to a given equation and forcing.

Lecture 7 showed that the solutions of an inhomogeneous linear equation are one particular solution plus the homogeneous solutions. In this lecture we construct the particular solution. Variation of parameters builds it from a fundamental system, for any continuous forcing and continuous, possibly variable, coefficients. Undetermined coefficients finds it by linear algebra, for constant coefficients and forcing by polynomials, exponentials, sines and cosines.

Summary

A particular solution and a fundamental system give every solution of a regular inhomogeneous second-order linear equation, and the initial data are imposed on their sum. Variation of parameters replaces the constants of the complementary function by functions. Its result is an integral of the forcing against a kernel. The kernel K(⋅,ξ)K(\cdot,\xi)K(⋅,ξ) is the homogeneous solution that starts at x=ξx=\xix=ξ with value zero and slope one, so it does not depend on the fundamental system. For constant coefficients and forcing by a polynomial times an exponential, the trial xkh(x)eσxx^kh(x)\ee^{\sigma x}xkh(x)eσx has exactly one solution. The power kkk is the multiplicity of σ\sigmaσ as a characteristic root.
