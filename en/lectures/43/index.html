A release at one point spreads under diffusion. A source that stays on adds new releases at every later time. Part IV begins by constructing the kernels that describe both effects. The stages are free diffusion and its initial trace (Section 43.1), bounded uniqueness and causal sources (Sections 43.2–43.4), and images and interval kernels (Sections 43.5–43.6). The last stage connects the whole line to Lecture 37’s finite rod.

Summary

A diffusion propagator carries initial data forward from one time. Its causal extension supplies the response to a source at each space-time point. The free Gaussian, the absorbing half-line kernel and the interval kernel serve different spatial boundary conditions.
